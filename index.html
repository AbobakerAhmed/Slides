Prompt Engineering

Clear instructions help the model understand what you want. They don’t guarantee a correct answer.

Four useful parts of a prompt

  • Task: what you want done
  • Context: relevant information and constraints
  • Format: length, structure, and language
  • Role, when useful: perspective or tone to adopt

What helps

  • Add examples when the output needs a specific style or structure.
  • For hard problems, request a brief explanation, assumptions, and supporting evidence.
  • Provide the relevant source material or data.
  • Check the answer, then refine instructions around specific errors.

Weak output can reflect an unclear prompt, missing information, or the model’s limitations.

When to use AI and when not to?

The line is whether you can check the answer.

Reach for it

  • Brainstorming and first drafts
  • Explaining unfamiliar code or a dense paper
  • Boilerplate, tests, refactors, regex
  • Rewriting and translating your own text
  • Rubber-ducking a bug at 2am

Keep it away

  • Facts you have no way to verify
  • Citations and numbers, it invents both
  • Private or client data you do not own
  • Work you must defend but cannot explain
  • Anything where being confidently wrong is expensive
DO NOT use it as an absolute source of truth.
